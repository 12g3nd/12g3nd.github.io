This field guide highlights a rather narrow slice of Earth, fairly unevenly. A little too personal and recent even. Vague and another adjective.

## 1. Arriving here

First, locate a roommate with a minivan. It makes the ride *much more* comfortable.

Ours leaves Toronto via the Gardiner with hyperpop and trap alternating at max volume, collects three people in Oakville and another in Waterloo, and arrives in London at about eleven on Friday night. All of these locations are in Southern Ontario.

We have covered very little (geographically) of the planet, but we have managed to pick up everyone.

## 2. Accommodation

You can stay at the house where most of your friends are staying. Or, to ease the sleeping situation, you can go to another close friend’s house, where you also know everybody, just not as well.

I choose the second house. We talk until five in the morning with some people I haven't seen in a long time.

At nine, I’m awake. Somehow. One of the guys makes us eggs and toast. The eggs are very good. Beer, eggs, and toast. Nine in the morning. That was mentioned already.

Leave your bags and clothes here. You will spend most of the afternoon elsewhere.

## 3. Identification

Your host may provide clothing appropriate to the occasion.

A Western Grandma shirt. Alright, buddy. Interesting attire for a nineteen-year-old man who attends the University of Toronto. I also have a purple bandana around my head. But you unlock that later in the day.

I wear this interesting outfit all day.

## 4. Local conditions

Spend some time in the backyard. It's nice, spacious even.

There is a game involving dice and a table that was made the day before. *Beer die*, I believe. It’s fun, although it would probably be more fun if I had better coordination. Would’ve rather played beer pong instead of beer die, honestly, but beer anything was fine with me. For a while, sitting on the lawn chair out there, I understand what people mean when they talk about going to a university town for undergrad. Everybody seems to have ended up in the same place.

I live in Toronto. I can see the CN Tower from my bed. I've grown fond of the city, especially right now, while I’m young and supposed to be reckless.

I would also quite like this backyard.

You may experience some difficulty wanting things that do not come with the place you otherwise want to be. I haven’t prepared a guide for that. Let me know if you do. I would really benefit from reading that.

## 5. The itinerary

Go out into the streets. Walk into a house party where you barely know anyone. Then text the friends you drove with originally. Walk around.

Walking around is fine, but after a while we are only walking around. We get burrito bowls and go back to the first house.

Two of the guys suggest basketball.

This wasn’t the reason we came to London. My outfit made that pretty clear. The court is about a ten-minute walk away, beside an elementary school. We can go anyway.

## 6. Conduct

Put your phone in the grass with everybody else’s and tighten the bandana and belt.

Stay on our half of the court. There are little kids playing on the other half, and we’re careful to give them their space and limit the cussing.

We play kind of physically. Small fouls and hand checks are mostly allowed to pass. You pass once before taking a shot. That sort of thing. There are rules, but they’re the sort everyone playing seems to understand already. Just don't play with the intention to break that shared understanding.

I like basketball a lot. I played growing up. Liked watching it too. I’m not the greatest shooter, but I try to lock in (at least a little).

I’ll explain more in a second. The ball is being checked to me.

## 7. A practical demonstration

I hit a three. Then I hit another. Two back-to-back threes to win the game.

I had a feeling the shots would go in, which is much easier to admit once they have.

It isn’t something I’m usually known for being able to do. At all. It was just something cool I did, and for a bit everyone was excited about it.

Please allow me a moment before we move on. I mean, I did allocate a whole section for it.

## 8. Other residents

Some older kids arrive on e-bikes. They’re loud and annoying, although we don’t actually talk to them, so I won't lie and give you a particularly thorough account.

We joke about hoping we weren’t like that at their age.

I *really* hope not.

For the record, this thought came from someone in a Western Grandma shirt.

## 9. Leaving

Eventually, people get tired. Nobody had expected to play basketball, and we have other plans. We’re supposed to get to a bar by eight because the queues get long.

One more game, then we go.

Back at the first house, everybody showers except me. I would really like to change. All my clothes are at the other house. Refer to section 2. I’ve been in this shirt all day, including basketball.

Fortunately, some of us go to the other house soon after. Unfortunately, getting drunk, acting the fool, and testing my limits weren’t part of the original plan. All in all, I had a good weekend trip. Should just be more careful next time.
