It's almost midnight and my AirPods are dead, so for once I'm just here. Stuck mainly with my thoughts and the occasional observation.

The train seems to be tired like me. You can hear the gears somewhere under the floor (at least that's where I assume they are — I'm not a train expert unfortunately), the creak of the car leaning into the track, the casual shudder when it decides to remind you of its purpose. A few rows away from me, there are some soccer fans, World Cup probably, but they're running on fumes too. The conversation has gone soft, the way voices do past a certain hour on a Sunday night. Perhaps it's the fact that I'm sitting on the small middle-section that connects the top and bottom floors, slightly away from everyone else, but just enough to get glimpses. Outside the window it's pitch black. Every so often a light slides by, a store sign, something, gone before I can process it. The rest of the time the window just gives me back my own reflection, lit by this warm-toned LED they've set to mimic the yellow of the fluorescent bulbs from before LEDs were even conceived. A fake version of an old light. I don't mind it. It's familiar.

I just passed Union.

I've got dry sweat on me from basketball and the particular tiredness of a two-hour commute done twice in one day, out and back. My soles and ribs ache a little, which is my body's way of telling me I need to go to the gym more and build some stamina I clearly don't have. But my head is clear. For someone who is stressed roughly all of the time, that's not nothing. I feel like everything is going to be alright. It seems contrary to my gut's usual instincts, but I let it go.

<p className="lede">That's the whole reason I like this as much as I do.</p>

I'm not doing anything. I'm waiting, and I know exactly what I have to do, which is nothing, which is wait. My bed is made at the other end of this. I had a good day. I had a coffee chat in Oakville with a friend's dad, and then ended up playing ball with my friends (and some peculiar strangers), which was the actual point even if it wasn't the plan. And now I'm here, suspended between the two, and I just know I'm going to be okay. The day is already a thing that happened.

Nobody else I know does this as often as I do (not yet, anyway). The two hours each way is mine alone, and somewhere along the line, I started to need it. It's not a spectacular thing. I know that. I'm a little at a loss to explain why a midnight commuter train I'm too tired to be on feels like one of the truest parts of my life. It's one of the biggest constants I have right now.

Right now being the operative part. Eventually I'll get comfortable enough on the highways and I'll drive to Oakville instead, because it's faster, and I'll trade this in without much ceremony. Which is maybe why I'm writing it down. You don't usually get to notice a constant while it's still happening. You only find out something was sacred after it's already gone.

Oakville does that to me too. Every time I'm back I get a tinge of nostalgia, a low ache for the things I'm not around for anymore. I traded all of that for quiet, and I'm still not sure it was a fair deal. The eleven years I spent there ended on the last day of high school. It'll always be my hometown at least. The train is the part in between, where I don't have to have decided yet. Old home behind me, new home ahead, and for forty minutes I'm just a guy in a yellow-lit window who doesn't have to be from anywhere.

Ajax is coming up. In a few minutes I'll get off, and the best part starts: my car, where the music finally works again, and the streets will be empty the way they only are this late. The same yellow I've been sitting under all night, except real this time with streetlights, one after another, the whole way home. My parents will be asleep. The house will be quiet. I'll let myself in without turning much on. Hoping I don't scare the cat.

And I'll go to bed, and it'll have been an ordinary day, and that, somehow, is the thing I wanted to keep.
